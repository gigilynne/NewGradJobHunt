Phase 2Every Week · No Exceptions

Apply — Every Single Week

Applying is not a one-time phase. It's the job until you have a job. Five applications every week, Tuesday and Thursday, recurring until you accept an offer.

🎯 Weekly Target: 5 Applications
Tuesday and Thursday are Apply Days. Log every application in the tracker below — company, role, date, status. The running total is the first thing reviewed at every check-in.

One-Time Strategy Setup
Apply the 60% rule — every time
Don't apply if you meet less than 60% of the requirements. For each application: one tailored line in the cover letter naming why that specific company. Quality matters — but still hit 5/week.
Memorize this rule — apply it every Tuesday and Thursday
Filter LinkedIn to mid-size companies (50–500 employees)
Mid-size companies hire faster, give new grads more responsibility, and are less competitive than large companies. Marketing agencies especially use data heavily. Set this filter and save your search.
Action: Set and save filter in LinkedIn

Phase 3Week 2–3 · 2 actions/week

Make Connections — Low Pressure

Start warm — people you already know or have something in common with. The goal is conversations, not job asks. Two actions per week, every week.

One-Time Setup
Email your university's career center
Most career centers help alumni for 1–2 years. Ask about: alumni job boards, employer connections in data/marketing, and mock interviews.
Deliverable: Email sent, reply noted
Join one online community in data or marketing
Pick one: r/datascience, r/analytics, Locally Optimistic (Slack), or Women in Data. Lurk a week, then introduce yourself in one sentence.
Deliverable: Joined and posted a brief intro
Recurring Weekly Networking
✅ This week: 2 LinkedIn connection requests (Wednesday)
Warm connections first: CS classmates, professors, club members. Note: "Hi [Name], we both studied CS at [School]. I just graduated and am looking for data analyst roles — would love to connect."

After someone accepts (wait 2–3 days): "Thanks for connecting! I'd love to hear about your experience in [field]. Would you be open to a quick 15-min chat?" Goal is to learn — not ask for a job.
⟳ 1 call per week by Week 3

Phase 5Week 2 onward · 20 min/week

Interview Prep — Before You Need It

Rehearsed answers reduce cognitive load when you're nervous — your brain retrieves instead of creates under pressure. Start now, not when interviews arrive.

One-Time Setup
Build your Interview Dossier doc
Google Doc: 1) My story — degree, projects, why CS/data/marketing. 2) My skills — tools you actually use. 3) My proudest moments — 3 specific things you built or solved. 4) My goals — 2 years from now.
Deliverable: Doc shared with mom
Write your "Tell me about yourself" answer
Past (CS degree, focus) → Present (looking for data/marketing roles, GitHub) → Future (growing toward PM). Write word-for-word, then practice until fluent — not memorized.
Deliverable: Written answer in dossier
Write 3 STAR stories from your projects
Situation · Task · Action · Result. Use GitHub projects — they count. Write 3 in your dossier. They cover 80% of behavioral questions and are reusable across interviews.
Deliverable: 3 STAR stories in dossier
Prep 3 smart questions to ask interviewers
Strong options: "What does success look like in the first 90 days?" / "What's the biggest challenge the team is working through?" / "What do people who thrive here have in common?"
Deliverable: 3 questions in dossier
Weekly Practice Rotation
Week 2
"Tell me about yourself." Past → Present → Future.
Week 3
"What's your greatest weakness?" Real one with a real fix.
Week 4
"Why do you want to work here?" Reference something specific.
Week 5
"Tell me about a challenge you overcame." Use a STAR story.
Week 6
"Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" Show ambition toward PM.
Week 7+
"Tell me about working on a team." Pull from class projects.
✅ This week: Practice 1 question out loud, record it (Wednesday)
Pick one question from the rotation above. Answer out loud. Record on your phone. Play it back once. One question, one recording, every Wednesday — 20 minutes.
⟳ Every Wednesday
📍 Phase 5 Check-In

Any real interviews scheduled? Use dossier to prep specifically.

Looking AheadMonth 2–6

Path Toward Project Management

The goal isn't just any job — it's the right first step toward where you actually want to go.

Add PM-bridge titles to your job alerts
Business Analyst · Marketing Coordinator · Operations Analyst · Program Coordinator. These roles involve owning data, working across teams, and contributing to decisions — all core PM skills.
Action: Add 4 titles to LinkedIn alerts
Bookmark: Google PM Certificate (after you're hired)
Practical, well-regarded, free to audit. Best done while working so the material connects to real situations. Don't do it now — bookmark for 3–6 months in. coursera.org/google-certificates
Bookmark for later
🚌 Your Bus Destination
Write one sentence describing what your work life looks like 18 months from now. Not a job title — a feeling and a direction. "I'm working at a company where I use data to help a team make better decisions, and I'm starting to take on projects that involve coordinating across teams." That's your destination. Every application either moves you toward it or it doesn't.
